## Audience and channel identity

Our audience hypothesis is people who enjoy choosing a country, following a clear competition, and watching satisfying simulated races. English titles and concise on-screen English are the initial choice because the existing catalogue already uses them. Keep the action understandable without dialogue; add other languages when viewer evidence justifies the work.

The recurring promise is: **Pick a country. Survive three elimination checkpoints. See who wins.** Each episode must work for someone who has never watched the channel. Explain the event in one sentence and make the remaining competitors easy to follow.

Keep both formats on @MarbleFormula for the pilot period, with separate playlist names, thumbnail treatments and explicit “Marble Race” or “Car Championship” wording. Do not spend this launch period building a second channel.

| Series | Initial allocation | Reason to return |
|---|---:|---|
| Marble Formula Knockout | Three of every four new main releases | Recognizable national competitors, three checkpoints, a distinctive track challenge and a complete result. |
| Marble Formula Cars | One of every four new main releases, once ready | A complete car event with coherent qualifying and final rules, rather than isolated development renders. |
| Shorts | Two supporting releases per main event | A clear contest or obstacle moment that leads naturally to the full event. |
| Studio and design stories | Occasional replacement for a Short | Show an obstacle being designed and then its actual effect in a race. Avoid interrupting the main show with long technical tutorials. |

The 75/25 split is a starting hypothesis, not a conclusion from the 16 views. If cars are not ready, use an approved marble event or move the slot. Do not release a faulty car race to maintain the ratio.

## Release rhythm

Start the regular rhythm after there are **two approved main episodes and four approved Shorts** in reserve, with production time measured for at least one complete event. If that buffer cannot be sustained, publish a main episode every other week and one Short per week until it can.

| Slot | Content | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Tuesday, 18:00 Europe/Berlin | Short from the previous completed event | Introduce a country battle or obstacle and point to that event. |
| Friday, 18:00 Europe/Berlin | One complete race event | Deliver the main competition and its result. |
| Sunday, 18:00 Europe/Berlin | A different Short from Friday’s event | Show a second genuine moment and invite viewers into the full event. |

18:00 is a convenient operating time, not a discovered audience optimum. Revisit it after audience data becomes useful. YouTube says publish time is not known to determine long-term video performance. A sustainable rhythm matters more than forcing daily uploads. [YouTube performance FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805?hl=en), [upload schedule guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13616979?hl=en).

Use normal scheduled releases initially. Consider a Premiere later if viewers actively want to watch and talk together. Avoid adding live broadcasts to an already unproven render workload.

## What each video must deliver

For newly authored main episodes, show the central challenge in the first few seconds without revealing the winner. Establish the countries and rules quickly, then start racing. Avoid a long logo sequence before viewers understand the event. Checkpoint graphics should explain who remains and what happens next. Finish with a readable result and one relevant next video.

Keep 4K UHD 60 fps for new main productions, readable mobile graphics, coherent camera coverage and sound that makes impacts and changes in position easy to follow. Describe the work accurately as simulated racing; do not market assisted arcade physics as certified real-world vehicle dynamics.

Each event needs a substantive creative difference: track layout, obstacle interaction, actual rivalry, tournament stakes, race coverage or an original design story. A large number of nearly interchangeable renders is not our growth plan. YouTube’s monetization policy specifically addresses repetitive or mass-produced content; original authorship alone does not establish that every repeated template adds value. [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en).

Shorts should initially test 15–35 seconds: immediate action, one understandable situation, a payoff, and a clear route to the related event. Create separate vertical productions from approved source scenes/telemetry or separately approved Short assets. Preserve every original master unchanged; do not crop, recompress or shorten a master as the production shortcut. Archive-derived Shorts need their source and editorial quality checked first.

Use YouTube’s Related Video link for each Short when advanced feature access is available. Choose the corresponding public or unlisted main video, not an unrelated latest upload. Availability must be checked in Studio; long-upload eligibility does not prove access to this feature. [Related Video guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14075157?hl=en).

Lead titles with the event and its interesting question; put episode or season numbering later. Reduce repeated all-caps prefixes and long strings of locations where they obscure the promise. Do not label the existing HD releases as 4K.

Draft title directions:

- New Alpine episode: “Can Your Country Survive the Alpine Knockout? | Marble Race”
- New vortex episode: “The Vortex Decides Who Survives | Marble Elimination Race” — only if the verified race supports that claim.
- Completed car tournament: “Swiss Alps Car Championship | Heats to Grand Final”
- Existing Season 1 Part 1: “Country Marble Elimination: Monza, Monaco & Silverstone | Part 1”

Thumbnails should show one recognizable obstacle or racing conflict, two or three readable competitors, and no more than a few words. Use consistent visual distinctions for marbles and cars. Prepare two genuinely different concepts, such as a country rivalry and an obstacle challenge, rather than tiny color variations.

Test one packaging question at a time when traffic is sufficient. Current Studio A/B testing supports titles, thumbnails or combinations, requires advanced features, and is not available for Shorts. Let the native test assess watch time rather than selecting a winner from a handful of clicks. At the current channel size, “insufficient evidence” is an acceptable outcome. [YouTube A/B testing guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400?hl=en).

The first description lines should explain this specific event. Then include accurate rules, chapters where useful, the series/next-video link, credits and the website. Use natural phrases such as “marble elimination race” or “simulated car championship”; do not spend the main effort on tag lists. YouTube says tags primarily help with spelling variants. [YouTube discovery guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805?hl=en).

Organize the six completed releases into a clear chronological path. Use a “Start Here” playlist, plus the relevant season/world-tour playlists. Choose Season 1 Part 1 as the provisional introduction because it is a clear entry point, not because three views establish it as the best performer. Link Part 1 to its corresponding Finale. Add the car playlist when a complete car release is ready. Keep existing video IDs and history; do not reupload the catalogue merely to change packaging.

## Production and publication gate

For each release, maintain a single record with episode ID, series, exact source file, source hash, duration/resolution/fps, simulation and render version, review state, title/thumbnail version, intended slot, YouTube ID, processing state and Cloud object generation after archival.

1. Finish and technically validate the local render. Resolve the applicable physics, fairness, titles, audio and recovery findings in the production audit.
2. Review the complete event together, including winner, elimination order, on-screen claims, sound, thumbnail and description. Approval must identify the exact final file and package.
3. After approval, upload separately with private/scheduled visibility as appropriate. Leave at least a day of operational slack; that is a buffer, not a processing-time guarantee.
4. Check YouTube processing, intended 4K availability, restrictions and playback before the public slot. Move the slot if verification is incomplete. Recheck current long-upload eligibility when relevant.
5. Publish at the agreed time, then archive the validated completed video after confirmed successful YouTube processing. Preserve the local master. No drafts or unvalidated renders go to Cloud automatically.

## Measurement and decisions

The user-confirmed primary outcome is growth in returning viewers over rolling 28-day windows, once Studio reports are populated. Evaluate the count alongside total audience size so that a rising returning-viewer share caused only by fewer new viewers is not mistaken for growth. Supporting outcomes are main-video watch time, subscribers gained by series, and evidence that viewers continue to another episode. Keep Shorts and main-video metrics separate.

Record views at 48 hours, 7 days and 28 days after publication. Once Analytics is available, also record impressions and CTR by relevant traffic source, average view duration/percentage viewed, early audience retention, end-screen continuation, subscriber gains and Shorts engaged-view/stayed-to-watch measures where available. Mark missing or privacy-limited metrics as unavailable, not zero. Do not divide cumulative lifetime views by age and call that a fair release comparison.

| Signal after enough comparable observations | Next decision |
|---|---|
| Low CTR but reasonable retention relative to our own similar episodes | Test a clearer title or thumbnail. Keep the race itself unchanged. |
| Clicks arrive but viewers leave before the race becomes clear | Improve the promise, opening and rule explanation in the next authored episode. |
| Viewers drop during repetitive coverage or results overlays | Improve future camera direction and presentation; preserve published and stored masters. |
| Shorts attract views but little evidence of continuing to the event | Make the Short’s subject and Related Video more closely connected. Do not increase Short volume solely for vanity views. |
| Car releases repeatedly underperform comparable marble releases | Keep cars as an occasional pilot and investigate differences before increasing their share. Two pilots alone are not a reliable genre verdict. |
| Impressions remain too small to compare | Continue controlled publication, collect more observations and seek specific viewer feedback. Do not claim an A/B winner. |

Use at least three comparable releases and fixed observation windows before a major format decision. This is an internal discipline, not a YouTube statistical threshold. Compare traffic mix and video length as well as results. Avoid universal CTR/retention targets or subscriber guarantees while the channel has only 16 reported views.

Also track human review time, render time, retries and disk use per approved episode. If the reserve falls below one approved main episode, reduce frequency instead of bypassing review. Increase to two main releases per week only after at least four sustainable weekly cycles and a replenishing reserve.

**Days 1–7:** inspect the three processing uploads in Studio; verify source identities for the six completed releases; prepare playlist/packaging changes for review; obtain Analytics reports or plan the read-only integration; fix the production blockers and measure one complete render. Confirm the target audience and language with actual viewer evidence when available.

Separate Shorts from old footage require their own source and quality approval. If the production gate is not met, keep preparing rather than forcing a launch date.

**Days 31–60:** continue the first marble/car cycle, review comparable seven-day results, and run one packaging experiment at a time if impressions support it. Each new event must improve one concrete aspect of clarity, racing or visual quality.

**Days 61–90:** use 28-day audience patterns, production cost and several comparable releases to choose the next series allocation. Keep one channel unless audience evidence and production capacity justify separation. Expand frequency only when the reserve and quality are stable.

For a full 12-week publishing period after readiness, the working capacity target is 12 main events and 24 Shorts. This is not a promise to produce them within 90 calendar days if setup or quality fixes take longer. The first success criterion is a repeatable, reviewed show with improving viewer response—not an arbitrary subscriber or revenue number.
